\#\# Value proposition and outcome

\*\*Primary value proposition:\*\* personal hearing-aid guidance in Victoria, from understanding hearing needs and discussing suitable options to fitting, learning and continued care.

\*\*Primary visitor outcome:\*\* work toward easier everyday communication and greater confidence participating in conversations. Individual experience varies; hearing aids do not restore normal hearing or solve every cause of hearing difficulty.

The page should make a first appointment feel understandable and worthwhile, without assuming the visitor needs to buy a device. Keep both the individual and an accompanying family member welcome.

\#\# Tone and headline philosophy

\- Warm, capable and plain-spoken. Emotional relevance opens the story; factual explanations carry it forward.  
\- Address adults respectfully. Avoid "the elderly," helplessness, infantilizing language and assumptions about age or family structure.  
\- Use everyday situations, such as following a conversation or asking for repetition, without dramatizing isolation or shame.  
\- Prefer concrete verbs and short sentences. Explain unfamiliar terms where needed; avoid degrees of hearing loss, audiogram detail and technical specifications in the main scan path.  
\- Avoid fear-based urgency, unsupported health consequences, superlatives, aggressive sales language and invented scarcity.  
\- Use qualified benefit wording where appropriate: "may help," "designed to," "options depending on your needs." Avoid making every sentence tentative when stating verified service facts.  
\- Let local reviews substantiate patience and helpfulness. Do not convert those experiences into universal service guarantees.

Hero headline direction: connect the possibility of easier conversations with explicit local hearing help. If the headline is primarily emotional, the service/location identifier must be immediately adjacent. A phrase such as "easier everyday conversations" is a theme, not a proven outcome for every visitor.

\#\#\# FAQ priorities

Write approximately six useful answers, typically 40–75 words where sufficient:

1\. \*\*Is the consultation free?\*\* Distinguish the supported consultation/complimentary screening from the full evaluation. Do not imply the latter is free.  
2\. \*\*What happens at an evaluation, and can someone come with me?\*\* Explain assessment, discussion, approximate duration and family participation.  
3\. \*\*How much do hearing aids cost, and are payment options available?\*\* Costs vary; discuss suitable options, inclusions and listed financing. Do not invent prices or imply financing equals insurance.  
4\. \*\*How do I know which style is suitable?\*\* Suitability includes hearing needs, dexterity, anatomy, lifestyle and budget; discreet options are conditional.  
5\. \*\*Will I get help learning to use and adjust to them?\*\* Explain instruction and follow-up, without promising immediate or universal success.  
6\. \*\*Can you help with hearing aids bought elsewhere?\*\* Most makes/models can be discussed for service; avoid unconfirmed service-plan prices or guaranteed repairs.

If insurance is added as a question, explicitly acknowledge that acceptance is unconfirmed and direct the visitor to ask Brown about their specific situation. Do not imply Brown has verified benefits-checking capability. Omit tinnitus, remote-care and house-call promises unless their scope and local availability are confirmed. Do not pad the FAQ to include every research topic.
