Common Signs of Hearing Loss

Hearing changes can be gradual. You or someone close to you may notice:

Asking people to repeat themselves

You miss words or rely on watching faces to follow a conversation.

Struggling in busy places

Restaurants and group conversations become harder to follow.

Turning the TV up

Others mention the volume, or you miss the phone or doorbell.

Noticing a change doesn’t mean you need hearing aids. An evaluation can help you understand your hearing and explore suitable next steps.

Fitting and ongoing support

At a separate fitting appointment, your devices are programmed for your hearing needs. Practice putting them in, taking them out, charging or changing batteries, and cleaning them. Follow-up visits provide fine-tuning, cleaning and maintenance as you adjust.

Brown lists lifetime device programming among its complimentary services. Ask what is included for your hearing aids; getting comfortable may take more than one appointment.

Already wear hearing aids?

Brown assesses repairs for most major makes and models and operates its own hearing aid laboratory and repair center at its Orange headquarters. Tell the Victoria team your brand and the issue to discuss eligibility, costs and next steps.

Appointment timing and what to bring

Appointment timing

Hearing Aid Options for Your Everyday Life

Compare six hearing-aid styles, then explore suitable options with your specialist.

BTE

Behind-the-ear

Sits behind the ear and connects to an earpiece through a tube. Larger housing can make handling easier.

RIC

Receiver-in-canal

A small unit behind the ear connects by a thin wire to a receiver inside the ear canal.

ITE

In-the-ear

A custom shell sits in the outer ear and can provide easier access to controls.

ITC

In-the-canal

Sits partly in the ear canal, balancing a smaller profile with handling and available controls.

CIC

Completely-in-canal

Fits within the ear canal for a less visible profile, with small components to handle.

IIC

Invisible-in-canal

Fits deeper in the ear canal for a discreet profile. Suitability depends on your hearing and ear anatomy.

Illustrations show general styles. Actual devices and fit vary.

Not sure which style to choose?

You do not need to choose before your appointment. Your specialist will help compare options for your hearing, comfort and budget. The smallest styles are not suitable for everyone.

Is the consultation free?

Yes. Brown offers a free consultation to discuss your hearing concerns and options, as well as complimentary hearing screenings. A full hearing evaluation is a separate service. When scheduling, confirm which service your visit includes and whether any charges apply.

What happens at my first visit?

Your first visit is a chance to discuss what you’ve noticed and ask questions. If a hearing evaluation is arranged, it includes tests of your hearing and speech understanding, followed by a discussion of the results and suitable next steps. Plan for up to an hour for the evaluation, plus discussion time. A family member is welcome.

What happens after I request an appointment?

Your details are sent to the Victoria office as an appointment request. Submitting the form does not reserve a time; your appointment needs confirmation from the office. To arrange a time by phone or check on your request, call 409-207-8726.

How much do hearing aids cost?

Costs depend on the technology and features you choose. Your specialist can discuss options around your hearing, lifestyle and budget. Brown’s pricing is all-inclusive; ask exactly what is covered for the devices you’re considering. Financing is available through CareCredit and Cherry, subject to each provider’s terms and approval.

Which hearing aid style is right for me?

Suitability depends on your hearing needs, ear anatomy, dexterity, vision, daily activities and preferences. Your specialist can explain the trade-offs between suitable styles, including discreet options. The smallest styles are not suitable for everyone.

Will I get help using my hearing aids?

Yes. At your fitting, you’ll practice putting your hearing aids in and taking them out, charging or changing batteries, and cleaning them. Follow-up visits allow fine-tuning as you adjust to new sounds; more than one adjustment may be needed. Written instructions are available on request, with regular cleanings and check-ins supporting ongoing care.

Can you service hearing aids bought elsewhere?

Brown services and repairs hearing aids from most major manufacturers, including devices bought elsewhere. Contact the office with your brand and the problem so the team can discuss what may be possible. Out-of-warranty repairs may involve a fee, and not every device can be repaired.
