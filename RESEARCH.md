## 2. SERVICE / TOPIC RESEARCH — Hearing Loss & Hearing Aids

### 2.1 Service description
- A hearing evaluation is described as the first step: it determines whether hearing loss exists and details its extent, type and specifics; performed in-office by a hearing instrument specialist (S7).
- The evaluation is a series of tests, including how well the person detects and understands speech; results guide treatment recommendations (S7).
- If tests show permanent hearing loss, hearing aid options are discussed; the specialist explains which sounds the patient struggles with and how devices could help (S12).
- Selection considers severity of loss, lifestyle, budget, hobbies/occupation; a questionnaire may be used; the final choice is the patient's (S12).
- Custom or earmold-based devices require ear impressions; devices are ordered from the manufacturer and programmed to the patient's prescription (S8, S12).

### 2.2 Conditions / problems addressed
- Hearing loss: sensorineural, conductive, mixed (S15, S16).
- Degrees: normal 0–25 dB HL, mild 26–40, moderate 41–70, severe 71–90, profound >91 (S15). S16 also references a "moderately-severe" category.
- Tinnitus (ringing in the ears) — listed as a possible symptom of sensorineural loss (S16); "Ringing in my ear / tinnitus or dizziness" is a contact-form reason on the Victoria page (S24). Tinnitus page (S25):
  - Described as ringing, buzzing, whooshing, hissing or clicking with no external source; constant or intermittent; can disrupt sleep.
  - Site states more than 50 million Americans experience tinnitus (undated stat).
  - Causes listed: loud noise, medication, hearing loss, ear infection, ear trauma, earwax buildup, Meniere's disease, vascular disorders; some causes (infection, wax) can be resolved easily.
  - Treatments listed: address the underlying cause (e.g., wax removal, medication change, antibiotics — note these are generic page statements; HIS scope does not include prescribing), behavioral therapy, **hearing aids with tinnitus-masking features (even without hearing loss)**, white noise machines, tinnitus retraining therapy (TRT).
  - ⚠ The page describes what a "hearing care provider" may recommend; it does not state that Brown itself provides TRT or behavioral therapy. Only "hearing aids with tinnitus masking" is clearly within Brown's offering.
- Broken/aging hearing aids, including devices bought elsewhere (S9, T3).
- Noise-exposure protection needs (musician, hunting, industrial — contact-form options) (S11, S24).

### 2.3 Symptoms mentioned (S15 unless noted — paraphrased)
- Others seem to mumble; frequently asking people to repeat themselves
- Laughing along at jokes without catching the details
- Needing others to fill in what was said in a meeting
- TV/radio louder than others prefer
- Missing the doorbell or phone
- Relying on watching faces to understand speech
- Missing environmental sounds (birds, leaves)
- Avoiding noisy restaurants
- Muffled or overly quiet sounds (conductive) (S16)
- Muffled speech, difficulty in background noise, clarity problems, tinnitus (sensorineural) (S16)
- Hearing loss is often gradual; others frequently notice before the person does (S15)

### 2.4 Causes mentioned
Aging, noise exposure, medications, infections, head/ear trauma, congenital/genetic factors, disease (S15). Conductive causes include earwax blockage, ear infections, perforated eardrum, otosclerosis, ossicle problems (S16).

### 2.5 Benefits mentioned
- Hearing aids are described as the most common and successful treatment for sensorineural loss, with settings adjustable as needs change (S16).
- Device goal: make everyday sounds audible and comfortable, clear and pleasant (S8).
- Starkey (manufacturer) features cited on the site: rechargeable up to 51 hours, water resistance/waterproof claims, app control, call streaming, find-lost-aids, TeleHear remote sessions, activity/cognitive tracking, fall detection (S14). **These are manufacturer claims; do not present as clinic outcomes.**
- General features mentioned: Bluetooth connectivity, directional microphones, noise/masking features, waterproof options (S7, S13, S15).
- Patient-reported benefits (testimonials, S21): hearing church services/sermons, lower TV volume, easier conversation with spouse, not feeling left out, comfort.

### 2.6 Treatment / service process (S6 — site's own 4-step process)
1. Come in for a hearing test → specialist determines whether hearing aids are the best solution.
2. Get matched with a device based on hearing loss, lifestyle and budget.
3. Customize and optimize — device tested and adjusted for fit.
4. Ongoing care — regular cleanings, maintenance, follow-ups.

Supporting detail:
- Evaluation typically lasts up to one hour plus discussion time; bringing a family member is recommended (S7).
- Fitting appointment generally takes at least one hour; specialist programs devices, teaches insertion/removal, charging/batteries, cleaning; written instructions can be requested (S8).
- Fitting is a starting point; further adjustments may take a few appointments; additional verification testing is done (S8).
- Adjustment period: positive attitude and family support help; it takes time to get used to sounds (S8).
- Ongoing care emphasized as essential (S3, S6).

### 2.7 Technologies / techniques / procedures mentioned
- Audiogram-based testing; speech testing (S15, S16)
- Otoscopic examinations (complimentary) (S1)
- Ear impressions for custom devices (S12)
- Prescription-based programming; lifetime device programming (complimentary) (S1, S8)
- Remote programming/telehealth fine-tuning (volume, noise reduction, directional mics, tone) (S10)
- Styles offered/explained: BTE, RIC, ITE, ITC, CIC, IIC (S13)
- Products named: Starkey Omega AI+, Starkey Edge AI (S14); Starkey and Thrive apps (S20)
- Signia products listed on S26 (manufacturer-style copy):
  - **MaX platform — Pure Charge & Go MaX:** AI-based sound processing ("Acoustic Intelligence"), follows multiple speakers in busy spaces, keeps own voice natural, adapts automatically without program switching, softens noise without muting voices.
  - **IX platform:** RealTime Conversation Enhancement (helps even when not facing the speaker); Pure Charge & Go IX / Pure BCT Charge & Go IX (MFi, ASHA, LE Audio, Bluetooth Classic; wireless charging case; app control); Styletto IX (slim, seven colors, rechargeable); Motion Charge & Go IX (rechargeable super power, telecoil); Active Pro IX / Active Mini IX (earbud look, instant in-office fit); Silk Charge & Go IX (ultra discreet, rechargeable); Insio IX (custom, battery-powered).
  - **Signia Assistant** app: AI-based personalization, 24/7 in-app support, streaming, remote control, troubleshooting.
  - ⚠ These are manufacturer feature claims; current stock/availability at the Victoria office is not stated. Client (from other Onspire builds) preferences on "AI" wording are not known for Brown — check before featuring AI language.
- Assistive listening devices: TV systems, phone amplifiers, alarm clocks, auditorium systems (S15)

### 2.9 Suitable candidates (as supported)
- Anyone noticing the symptoms in 2.3, or whose family notices them (S15).
- Hearing aid style suitability by degree of loss: BTE mild–profound; RIC mild–severe; ITE mild–severe; ITC/CIC/IIC mild–moderate (S13).
- Existing hearing aid users needing repair/adjustment, including devices not fit by Brown (S9, T3).
- People with limited mobility or who can't reach the office → remote care / house calls (S2, S10).
- Musicians/noise-exposed people → protection (S11).

### 2.10 Limitations, caveats, disclaimers
- Conductive loss may be temporary; some cases are medically correctable; hearing aids recommended for long-standing/permanent cases (S16). → Page should not imply hearing aids fix all loss.
- Sensorineural loss is generally permanent; may worsen; needs routine monitoring (S16).
- Remote/online test accuracy can vary (S10).
- Device trial: only "in some instances" can patients test devices (S12). **No trial period or return policy stated.**
- Warranty: new aids come with a warranty; out-of-warranty repairs possible for a small fee; some repairs may carry a new year-long warranty depending on manufacturer (S9). No specific Brown warranty terms.
- **Service plans for hearing aids bought elsewhere (T3 — unlinked page, currency unknown):** Bronze $299 (1 year, up to 4 visits), Silver $499 (3 years, up to 4 visits/yr), Gold $599 (unlimited visits for the life of the aids); each includes programming, cleaning and an ear health check; parts/accessory and non-warranty manufacturer repair fees may apply; eligibility requires HIS confirmation, aids 5 years old or less, suitable for current loss, in working order. The page's office list does **not** include Victoria. ⚠ Do not publish these prices without client confirmation.
- Healthcare disclaimer on the site: website information is not medical advice; visitors suspecting a hearing problem should consult a qualified physician or hearing professional (S29). Reusable as a footer/FAQ disclaimer.
- HIS scope: basic hearing tests for hearing aid candidacy (S15) — avoid "diagnostic audiology" or "medical diagnosis" language.

### 2.11 Useful patient education points
- People wait an average of seven years before seeking treatment (S15).
- Nearly 20% of US adults (48 million) report some hearing loss (S15) — site stat, undated source; use cautiously.
- Sensorineural loss accounts for >90% of hearing-aid wearers' loss (S15).
- Hearing loss is "a family issue" — bring a loved one (S7).
- Cost depends on technology level and features, not style (S13).
- Daily care: wipe with dry cloth, remove batteries overnight, inspect for damage (S9).
